Why this model is different

Method 1 infers the state from movement and tracking. Method 2 learns from four labelled matches, selects settings on a fifth, and is tested on two more. Barcelona public labels never select a model.

120 features connect ball-control memory, team geometry and movement. Boosted trees estimate the state; a sequence decoder turns those estimates into possessions.

The EDA story

When the tracker loses the ball

The tactical tracker loses the ball on 40.7% of graded frames. Almost all missing time—99.7%—comes from gaps longer than five seconds. That makes this a problem of understanding play across long interruptions in visibility.

The new model learns from other games where possession and dead-ball status are known. We remove stretches of ball tracking and some player observations, then ask it to recover the state. Team movement helps distinguish a stoppage from an occlusion; the last and next credible touches help bridge loose balls and passes. The sequence model discourages invented, flickering possessions.

Automatic tactical tags were explored but not added to the final classifier: agreement varied substantially by tag, and external training matches do not provide the same tagging system.
